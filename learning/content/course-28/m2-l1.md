Ask a friend to stand "over there" and they'll say "where?" A computer can't even ask. It needs an exact address. In Scratch, every spot on the stage has an address made of two numbers, and once you can read them, you can send a sprite anywhere.

Here's a script that walks the cat around the whole stage in a rectangle. By the end of this page, every number in it will make sense to you:

```scratch title="A trip around the edge of the stage"
when green flag clicked
go to x: (-200) y: (-120)
glide (1) secs to x: (200) y: (-120)
glide (1) secs to x: (200) y: (120)
glide (1) secs to x: (-200) y: (120)
glide (1) secs to x: (-200) y: (-120)
```

## Sprites act, the stage holds the scene

A **sprite** is anything that can move or do things: a character, a ball, a button, a cloud. Each sprite has its own scripts, its own costumes (pictures), and its own sounds. When you click a sprite in the sprite pane, you see *its* code.

The **stage** is the background that all the sprites stand on. It can have scripts too, but it can't move around. Instead of costumes it has **backdrops**.

To add a sprite, hover over the cat-face button at the bottom of the sprite pane:

-   **Choose a Sprite** opens the library, full of ready-made characters and objects.
-   **Paint** opens a blank costume so you can draw your own.
-   **Surprise** picks a random sprite for you.
-   **Upload Sprite** adds a picture from your computer.

## The stage is a map with x and y

The stage is 480 steps wide and 360 steps tall. Every spot on it has two numbers:

-   **x** tells you how far left or right. The left edge is `-240`, the middle is `0`, and the right edge is `240`.
-   **y** tells you how far up or down. The bottom edge is `-180`, the middle is `0`, and the top edge is `180`.

```mermaid caption="The stage as a map: the middle is (0, 0), and negative numbers mean left or down"

    x-axis "Left edge, x = -240" --> "Right edge, x = 240"
    y-axis "Bottom edge, y = -180" --> "Top edge, y = 180"
    quadrant-1 "x positive, y positive"
    quadrant-2 "x negative, y positive"
    quadrant-3 "x negative, y negative"
    quadrant-4 "x positive, y negative"
    "Middle (0, 0)": [0.5, 0.5]
    "Cat at (120, 90)": [0.75, 0.75]
    "Frog at (-180, -100)": [0.125, 0.22]
    "Bird at (-150, 140)": [0.19, 0.89]
```

People write a spot as **(x, y)**, x first. So `(120, 90)` means 120 to the right of the middle and 90 up. Two tricks for remembering which is which: **x goes across** (say "x is a cross"), and **y reaches up to the sky**. And whenever you see a minus sign, think "left" for x or "down" for y.

You don't have to guess the numbers. Drag a sprite around the stage and watch the **x** and **y** boxes in the sprite pane change as it moves. Want to see them on the stage itself? In **Motion**, tick the checkboxes next to `x position` and `y position` at the bottom of the list.

:::try
Before you drag anything, guess: where is `(0, 180)`? Where is `(-240, -180)`? Now drag the cat to each spot and read the boxes. (The cat can't go *exactly* to an edge, because Scratch keeps part of every sprite on the stage, but you'll get close.)
:::

## Four ways to move

Motion blocks are blue. These are the ones you'll use the most:

| Block | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `go to x: (0) y: (0)` | Jumps to a spot instantly, like teleporting |
| `glide (1) secs to x: (0) y: (0)` | Slides to a spot, taking that many seconds |
| `change x by (10)` | Shifts right (or left, with a negative number) |
| `change y by (10)` | Shifts up (or down, with a negative number) |
| `set x to (0)` | Jumps sideways to that x and keeps the same y |
| `move (10) steps` | Walks forward in whatever direction the sprite is facing |
| `turn right (15) degrees` | Spins clockwise |
| `point in direction (90)` | Faces a direction |

Now look at the rectangle script at the top again. It starts with `go to`, which plants the cat in the bottom-left corner at `(-200, -120)`. Each `glide` then slides it to the next corner: across to the right (x changes, y stays), up (y changes), back to the left, and down to where it started. Four glides of one second each, so the trip takes four seconds.

### Teleport or walk?

`go to` is a teleport: no time passes, and you never see the trip. `glide` is a walk: you watch the sprite travel. Use `go to` to put a sprite on its starting mark, and `glide` when the movement is part of the show.

### Directions and `move`

`move (10) steps` doesn't know about left or right. It walks forward, wherever the sprite is facing. Directions in Scratch are measured like a compass, in degrees:

| Direction | Faces |
| --- | --- |
| `90` | right (every new sprite starts here) |
| `-90` | left |
| `0` | up |
| `180` | down |

So `point in direction (-90)` followed by `move (50) steps` sends the cat 50 steps to the left. `change x by (-50)` gets it to the same spot without turning at all.

### Why so many scripts start with `go to`

Scratch remembers where a sprite was when the last run ended. Suppose your script only says `change x by (50)`. The first click moves the cat 50 steps right. The second click moves it 50 more, then 50 more, until it's squashed against the right edge. Putting `go to x: () y: ()` at the top sends it back to the starting line every single time.

## Costumes: a sprite's flipbook

A **costume** is one picture of a sprite. The cat comes with two, called `costume1` and `costume2`, and its legs are in different places in each. Flip between them quickly and the cat looks like it's walking, just like the drawings in the corner of a flipbook.

```mermaid caption="The cat's two costumes, and how next costume flips between them"
stateDiagram-v2
    direction LR
    costume1 --> costume2: next costume
    costume2 --> costume1: next costume
```

Open the **Costumes** tab to see them. You can paint on them, recolor them, or add new ones. Here's a three-step walk:

```scratch title="Three steps with moving legs"
when green flag clicked
switch costume to (costume1 v)
move (20) steps
wait (0.3) seconds
next costume
move (20) steps
wait (0.3) seconds
next costume
move (20) steps
```

The cat steps right three times and its legs change between each step. The `wait (0.3) seconds` blocks come from **Control**, and they matter more than they look. Scratch runs blocks so fast that, without waits, every costume change would happen before the screen redraws, and you'd see the cat jump to the end with no walking at all.

Next week, loops will repeat this for us so we don't have to stack the same blocks again and again.

## Backdrops change the scene

A **backdrop** is like a costume for the stage. Click **Choose a Backdrop** at the bottom right of the editor to add one. Any sprite can switch the backdrop, which is how a story moves from one scene to the next:

```scratch
when green flag clicked
switch backdrop to (backdrop1 v)
say [Let's go to the beach!] for (2) seconds
next backdrop
```

If your project has a second backdrop, the scene changes right after the cat finishes talking. With only one backdrop, nothing seems to happen, so add another before you test it.

## Looks blocks change how a sprite appears

Looks blocks are purple. Here are the ones you'll reach for most:

-   `say [Hi!] for (2) seconds` and `think [Hmm...] for (2) seconds` show speech and thought bubbles.
-   `change size by (10)` and `set size to (100) %` make a sprite grow or shrink.
-   `change [color v] effect by (25)` tints the sprite. Click the dropdown for other effects: `ghost` (see-through), `brightness`, `fisheye`, `whirl`, `pixelate`, and `mosaic`.
-   `clear graphic effects` turns every effect off.
-   `show` and `hide` make a sprite appear and disappear.
-   `go to [front v] layer` puts a sprite in front of all the others.

```scratch title="The cat eats something strange"
when green flag clicked
set size to (100) %
clear graphic effects
think [I feel funny...] for (2) seconds
change size by (50)
change [color v] effect by (50)
say [Whoa! I'm huge and green!] for (2) seconds
```

A thought bubble appears, then the cat grows to 150% and turns green, then it talks. Now look at the first two blocks under the hat. Sizes and effects **stay** after a project stops, so without those two reset blocks the cat would be 200% and a new color on the second run, 250% on the third, and so on.

## What usually goes wrong

:::warning
**Mixing up x and y.** If your sprite goes up when you wanted it to go right, you changed y instead of x. Say it out loud: "x is across, y is up."
:::

-   **Forgetting the minus sign.** `change x by (-10)` goes left. `change x by (10)` goes right.
-   **Effects that pile up.** Size and effects survive between runs. Reset them at the top of your script.
-   **`move` going the "wrong" way.** `move` follows the sprite's direction. If the cat is facing left, `move (10) steps` goes left.
-   **Editing the wrong sprite.** The Costumes tab only shows the selected sprite's costumes. Check which sprite is highlighted.

## Check yourself

Each script below starts with the cat in the middle, at `(0, 0)`, facing right at size 100%. Predict, then answer.

```scratch title="Script A"
when green flag clicked
go to x: (0) y: (0)
change x by (100)
change y by (-50)
```

```scratch title="Script B"
when green flag clicked
go to x: (0) y: (0)
point in direction (-90)
move (50) steps
```

```scratch title="Script C"
when green flag clicked
change size by (20)
```

:::quiz
Q: Where does the cat end up after **Script A**?
- [x] (100, -50)
  `change x by (100)` moves it right to x = 100, then `change y by (-50)` moves it down to y = -50.
- [ ] (-50, 100)
  That swaps x and y. The first change is to x, the second is to y.
- [ ] (100, 50)
  The minus sign on -50 means down, so y becomes negative.
- [ ] (0, 0)
  The `go to` only sets the start. The two `change` blocks still move it.

Q: Where does the cat end up after **Script B**?
- [ ] (50, 0)
  That's where it would go facing right. `point in direction (-90)` turned it to face left first.
- [x] (-50, 0)
  `move` walks forward in the direction the sprite faces. It's facing left, so 50 steps takes x from 0 to -50.
- [ ] (0, -50)
  Direction -90 is left, not down. Down is 180.

Q: You click the green flag on **Script C** three times. How big is the cat now?
- [ ] 120%
  That would be true if the size reset each time, but nothing in this script resets it.
- [x] 160%
  Size stays between runs: 100 becomes 120, then 140, then 160. Adding `set size to (100) %` at the top would fix it.
- [ ] 100%
  `change size by (20)` really does change the size, and it isn't undone when the project stops.

Q: You want the cat to slowly drift across the stage so the audience watches it travel. Which block fits best?
- [ ] `go to x: (200) y: (0)`
  `go to` teleports. The cat would just appear at the end.
- [x] `glide (3) secs to x: (200) y: (0)`
  `glide` shows the whole trip, and the number of seconds sets how slow it looks.
- [ ] `set x to (200)`
  Like `go to`, this jumps instantly.
:::

:::takeaways
- The stage is 480 by 360. The middle is `(0, 0)`, x runs from -240 to 240, and y runs from -180 to 180.
- `go to` teleports, `glide` shows the trip, and `change x by` and `change y by` shift a sprite without turning it.
- `move` walks in whatever direction the sprite faces: 90 is right, -90 left, 0 up, 180 down.
- Costumes are a sprite's flipbook. A `wait` between costume changes makes the animation visible.
- Position, size, and effects stay after a run ends, so start scripts with reset blocks.
:::
