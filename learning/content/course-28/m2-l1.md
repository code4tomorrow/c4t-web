How does Scratch know where "over there" is? You can't tell a computer "move a bit to the left, near the tree." You need an exact address, like a spot on a treasure map. This week you'll learn Scratch's map, then use it to move sprites anywhere and change how they look.

## Sprites and the stage

A **sprite** is anything that can move or do things: a character, a ball, a button, a cloud. Each sprite has its own scripts, costumes, and sounds.

The **stage** is the background where sprites live. The stage can have scripts too, but it can't move. It changes by switching **backdrops**.

You can add sprites three ways with the cat-face button at the bottom of the sprite pane:

-   **Choose a Sprite** opens the library, full of ready-made sprites.
-   **Paint** opens the paint editor so you can draw your own.
-   **Surprise** picks a random sprite.

## The stage is a map

The stage is 480 steps wide and 360 steps tall. Every spot has two numbers:

-   **x** says how far left or right. The left edge is `-240`, the middle is `0`, the right edge is `240`.
-   **y** says how far up or down. The bottom is `-180`, the middle is `0`, the top is `180`.

```text
               (0, 180) top
                   |
(-240, 0) left ----+---- right (240, 0)
                   |
              (0, -180) bottom

The middle of the stage is (0, 0).
```

A quick way to remember: **x goes across** (like the letter x lies flat), **y goes up to the sky**. Negative numbers mean left or down.

:::tip
Move your mouse over the stage while you're in the **Sensing** category. The `mouse x` and `mouse y` blocks let you check the numbers. Easier still: drag a sprite and watch its x and y change in the sprite pane.
:::

## Motion blocks

Motion blocks (blue) move and turn sprites. Here are the ones you'll use most:

| Block | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `move (10) steps` | Walks forward in the direction the sprite faces |
| `turn right (15) degrees` | Spins clockwise |
| `go to x: (0) y: (0)` | Jumps instantly to a spot |
| `glide (1) secs to x: (0) y: (0)` | Slides smoothly to a spot |
| `change x by (10)` | Moves right (or left, with a negative number) |
| `change y by (10)` | Moves up (or down, with a negative number) |
| `set x to (0)` | Jumps left/right but keeps the same y |
| `point in direction (90)` | Faces a direction: 90 right, -90 left, 0 up, 180 down |

Try this script with the cat:

```scratch
when green flag clicked
go to x: (-200) y: (-120)
glide (1) secs to x: (200) y: (-120)
glide (1) secs to x: (200) y: (120)
glide (1) secs to x: (-200) y: (120)
glide (1) secs to x: (-200) y: (-120)
```

What happens:

```text
The cat jumps to the bottom-left corner,
then slides right, up, left, and down,
tracing a big rectangle around the stage. It takes 4 seconds.
```

### "Go to" versus "glide"

`go to` is like teleporting: no time passes. `glide` is like walking: you see the trip. Use `go to` to set a starting spot, and `glide` when you want the movement to be part of the show.

### Why start with a "go to"?

Scratch remembers where a sprite was when the last run ended. If your script only moves the cat right, it will march a bit further right every time you click the flag, until it's stuck at the edge. Starting with `go to x: () y: ()` puts it back at the starting line every time.

## Costumes

A **costume** is one picture of a sprite. The cat has two: `costume1` and `costume2`. Its legs are in different spots in each one. Flip between them quickly and the cat looks like it's walking.

Open the **Costumes** tab to see them. You can draw on them, change colors, or add new ones.

```scratch
when green flag clicked
switch costume to (costume1 v)
move (20) steps
wait (0.3) seconds
next costume
move (20) steps
wait (0.3) seconds
next costume
move (20) steps
```

What happens:

```text
The cat takes three steps to the right,
switching legs each step, like it's walking.
```

The `wait (0.3) seconds` block comes from **Control**. Without it, the costume changes happen too fast to see. Next week, loops will let us repeat this without stacking the same blocks again and again.

## Backdrops

A **backdrop** is a costume for the stage. Add one with the **Choose a Backdrop** button at the bottom right. A sprite can switch the backdrop too, which is great for stories that change scenes.

```scratch
when green flag clicked
switch backdrop to (backdrop1 v)
say [Let's go to the beach!] for (2) seconds
next backdrop
```

If your project has a second backdrop, the scene changes after the cat speaks. If there's only one, nothing seems to happen, so add a second one first.

## Looks blocks

Looks blocks (purple) change how a sprite appears:

-   `say [Hi!] for (2) seconds` and `think [Hmm...] for (2) seconds` show bubbles.
-   `change size by (10)` and `set size to (100) %` make sprites grow or shrink.
-   `change [color v] effect by (25)` tints the sprite. Other effects in the dropdown include `ghost` (see-through), `fisheye`, `whirl`, and `pixelate`.
-   `clear graphic effects` puts everything back to normal.
-   `show` and `hide` make sprites appear and disappear.
-   `go to [front v] layer` puts a sprite in front of all the others.

```scratch
when green flag clicked
set size to (100) %
clear graphic effects
think [I feel funny...] for (2) seconds
change size by (50)
change [color v] effect by (50)
say [Whoa! I'm big and green!] for (2) seconds
```

What happens:

```text
Thought bubble: "I feel funny..." (2 seconds)
The cat gets 50 percent bigger and turns green.
Speech bubble: "Whoa! I'm big and green!" (2 seconds)
```

The first two blocks reset the cat so it doesn't keep growing every time you click the flag.

## What usually goes wrong

-   **Mixing up x and y.** If your sprite goes up when you wanted right, you changed y instead of x.
-   **Forgetting the minus sign.** `change x by (-10)` moves left. `change x by (10)` moves right.
-   **Effects that pile up.** Size and color effects stay after the project stops. Reset them at the start of your script.
-   **Editing the wrong sprite.** The Costumes tab shows costumes for the selected sprite only. Check which sprite is highlighted.
