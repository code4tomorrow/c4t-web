Every morning you make little decisions. *If* it's raining, take an umbrella; *else*, grab your sunglasses. Games make decisions like that all the time, many times every second. *If* the player touches the coin, collect it. *If* the ball reaches the wall, bounce. *If* the hero touches lava, start over.

Here's a cat that makes a decision about your mouse. Hover over it and it blushes and says hi. Move away and it goes back to normal:

```scratch title="The shy cat"
when green flag clicked
forever
    if <touching (mouse-pointer v)?> then
        say [Hi there!]
        set [color v] effect to (50)
    else
        say []
        clear graphic effects
    end
end
```

By the end of this page you'll know what every piece of that does, and how to use the same pattern for walls, coins, and finish lines.

## Yes-or-no questions

Some blocks have pointed ends, like a stretched hexagon. They're called **boolean** blocks, or conditions. They don't *do* anything by themselves. They answer a yes-or-no question with `true` or `false`.

From **Sensing**:

-   `<touching (mouse-pointer v)?>` asks: is this sprite touching the mouse pointer? (The dropdown also lists `edge` and every other sprite.)
-   `<touching color [#ff0000]?>` asks: is this sprite touching that color?
-   `<key (space v) pressed?>` asks: is that key being held down right now?
-   `<mouse down?>` asks: is the mouse button pressed?

From **Operators**, comparing numbers or words:

-   `<(x position) > (200)>` asks: is x bigger than 200?
-   `<(x position) < (-200)>` asks: is x smaller than -200?
-   `<(answer) = [yes]>` asks: is the answer exactly "yes"?

Click any boolean block in the palette and Scratch shows a little bubble saying `true` or `false` right now. Try it with `touching (mouse-pointer v)?`: set its dropdown to `edge` and click it while the cat is in the middle of the stage. Then drag the cat against a side of the stage and click it again.

## `if then`: run blocks only sometimes

The `if <> then` block in **Control** has a pointed hole in it, the same shape as a boolean block. Drop a condition into the hole. The blocks inside the `if` run only when the condition is true. When it's false, Scratch skips them.

An `if` checks **once**, at the moment Scratch reaches it. That's why the shy cat's `if` sits inside a `forever` loop: the loop sends Scratch back to ask the question again and again, many times a second.

```mermaid caption="The forever-if pattern: keep asking, and act only when the answer is yes"
flowchart TD
    A["forever"] --> B{"Is the condition true?"}
    B -->|yes| C["Run the blocks inside the if"]
    B -->|no| D["Skip them"]
    C --> A
    D --> A
```

This "forever-if" pattern is how almost every game watches for things to happen. You'll use it in every project from here on.

## `if else`: pick one of two paths

The bigger `if <> then` block has a second mouth labeled `else`. Every time Scratch reaches it, **exactly one** of the two parts runs: the top part if the condition is true, the bottom part if it's false. Never both, never neither.

Look at the shy cat again. With only an `if`, the cat would say "Hi there!" when you hovered, but the bubble would stay forever after you moved away, because nothing tells it to stop. The `else` part handles "not touching": `say []` with an empty bubble clears the speech, and `clear graphic effects` puts the color back.

## Smooth movement with `key pressed?`

Last week you moved the cat with `when [right arrow v] key pressed`. It works, but you noticed the pause before the key starts repeating. Games use a `forever` loop with `key pressed?` instead:

```scratch title="Smooth four-way movement"
when green flag clicked
go to x: (0) y: (0)
forever
    if <key (right arrow v) pressed?> then
        change x by (5)
    end
    if <key (left arrow v) pressed?> then
        change x by (-5)
    end
    if <key (up arrow v) pressed?> then
        change y by (5)
    end
    if <key (down arrow v) pressed?> then
        change y by (-5)
    end
end
```

Now the cat glides smoothly the instant you press a key, with no pause. And since all four `if`s are checked on every pass, holding up and right together moves it diagonally.

## Ask a question, use the answer

`ask [What's your name?] and wait` shows a question with a text box at the bottom of the stage, and the script waits until the player types something and presses Enter. Whatever they typed is stored in the `answer` block (in Sensing), which you can use like any other value:

```scratch title="A pizza survey"
when green flag clicked
ask [Do you like pizza? (yes or no)] and wait
if <(answer) = [yes]> then
    say [Me too!] for (2) seconds
else
    say [More for me, then!] for (2) seconds
end
```

Type `yes` and the cat says "Me too!" Type `no`, or anything else at all, and it says "More for me, then!" The `=` block ignores capital letters, so `YES` and `Yes` count as yes. Spelling still matters, though: `yeah` is not `yes`.

## Collisions: when things touch

A **collision** is when two things on the stage overlap. Scratch gives you three main ways to notice one.

### Touching another sprite

Pick the other sprite's name in the `touching` dropdown. Here the cat chases your mouse, and when you lead it to a sprite called **Apple**, it eats:

```scratch title="On the cat: chase and eat"
when green flag clicked
forever
    point towards (mouse-pointer v)
    move (3) steps
    if <touching (Apple v)?> then
        say [Yum!] for (1) seconds
        go to (random position v)
    end
end
```

After each bite, the cat jumps to a random spot, so you have to lead it back.

### Touching a color

`touching color` is perfect for walls, lava, and finish lines painted onto the backdrop. To set the color, click the color circle in the block, click the **eyedropper** at the bottom of the color menu, and then click that color on the stage. The eyedropper picks the exact color, which matters: a red that's slightly different won't count.

```scratch
if <touching color [#ff0000]?> then
    go to x: (-200) y: (0)
end
```

If the sprite touches red, it goes back to the start. Like every check, it needs to live inside a `forever` loop to keep watching.

### Touching the edge, or an invisible wall

`<touching (edge v)?>` turns true when any part of the sprite reaches the side of the stage. For a wall that isn't at the edge, check the sprite's position yourself:

```scratch title="Glass wall at x = 200"
when green flag clicked
forever
    if <key (right arrow v) pressed?> then
        change x by (5)
    end
    if <(x position) > (200)> then
        set x to (200)
    end
end
```

The cat walks right but stops dead at x = 200, as if it bumped into glass. Every time it steps past, the second `if` puts it back on the line before the screen even redraws.

:::tip
`if on edge, bounce` is a handy shortcut, but it only works for the four edges of the stage. To bounce off a paddle or a wall sprite, check `if <touching (Paddle v)?> then` and turn the sprite yourself.
:::

## What usually goes wrong

:::warning
**The `if` only works once.** You forgot the `forever` loop around it, so Scratch asked the question one time, at the start, and never again. Wrap the `if` in a `forever`.
:::

-   **`touching color` never turns true.** The colors don't match exactly. Use the eyedropper instead of picking a color by eye.
-   **The sprite gets stuck in a wall.** After a collision, move it back out (for example, `change x by (-5)`), so it isn't still touching the wall on the next check.
-   **It seems like both parts of an if/else ran.** You probably used two separate `if` blocks instead of one `if else`. Two `if`s can both run. The two halves of an `if else` never can.
-   **The sprite's own name isn't in the `touching` dropdown.** A sprite can't check whether it's touching itself, so its own name never appears. If you see the wrong list, you're editing the wrong sprite.

## Check yourself

```scratch title="Script A"
when green flag clicked
if <touching (mouse-pointer v)?> then
    say [Hi!] for (2) seconds
end
```

```scratch title="Script B"
when green flag clicked
forever
    if <key (space v) pressed?> then
        change size by (5)
    else
        set size to (100) %
    end
end
```

```scratch title="Script C"
when green flag clicked
ask [What is 2 + 2?] and wait
if <(answer) = [4]> then
    say [Correct!] for (2) seconds
else
    say [Try again.] for (2) seconds
end
```

:::quiz
Q: With **Script A**, the mouse is far from the cat when you click the flag. A few seconds later you move the mouse onto the cat. What happens?
- [ ] The cat says "Hi!"
  It would, if the `if` were inside a `forever` loop. Here it was checked only once.
- [x] Nothing happens
  The `if` asked its question at the moment the flag was clicked. The answer was false, the script ended, and nobody asks again.
- [ ] The cat says "Hi!" over and over
  There's no loop, so nothing repeats.

Q: With **Script B**, you hold space for one second and then let go. What do you see?
- [x] The cat keeps growing while you hold space, then snaps back to normal size the moment you let go
  While space is held, the top part runs on every pass and adds 5 each time. Once you let go, the `else` part runs instead and sets the size to 100.
- [ ] The cat grows by 5 once
  The `if` is inside a `forever`, so it adds 5 on every pass while the key is held.
- [ ] The cat stays big after you let go
  The `else` part runs as soon as space isn't pressed, and it resets the size.

Q: With **Script C**, the player types `four`. What does the cat say?
- [ ] "Correct!"
  Scratch doesn't turn words into numbers. To the `=` block, `four` and `4` are different.
- [x] "Try again."
  The answer `four` isn't equal to `4`, so the condition is false and the `else` part runs.
- [ ] Nothing, because the answer isn't a number
  Any answer works. It just goes to the `else` part.

Q: Your maze walls are blue rectangles painted on the backdrop. Which condition tells the player it hit a wall?
- [ ] `<touching (edge v)?>`
  `edge` means only the four sides of the stage, not walls drawn inside it.
- [x] `<touching color [#0000ff]?>`
  The walls are part of the backdrop, not a sprite, so you detect them by their color.
- [ ] `<touching (Wall v)?>`
  There's no sprite called Wall. The walls are painted on the backdrop, so they won't show up in this dropdown.
:::

:::takeaways
- Boolean blocks have pointed ends and answer yes-or-no questions with `true` or `false`.
- `if <> then` runs its blocks only when the condition is true. With `else`, exactly one of the two parts runs.
- An `if` checks once. Put it inside `forever` to keep checking: that's the forever-if pattern.
- `key pressed?` in a forever loop gives smooth movement with no key-repeat pause.
- `ask and wait` stores what the player types in `answer`, and `=` ignores capital letters.
- Detect collisions with `touching (sprite)?`, `touching color?` (use the eyedropper), `touching (edge)?`, or by checking `x position` and `y position`.
:::
