In the next lesson your laptop will learn to tell a thumbs-up from a thumbs-down. It will take about five minutes, it won't need a line of code, and the "learning" will happen inside one browser tab while you watch.

The tool that makes this possible is **Teachable Machine**, a free website from Google. You show it examples, it trains a small machine learning model on them, and then it tries to recognize new things you show it. We'll use it all course long to test ideas for real instead of just reading about them: why a model fails on a new background, why more examples help, why a perfect score can be a lie.

This lesson gets it running and makes sure you understand what happens to the pictures you take.

## What you need (and what you don't)

-   **A laptop or desktop computer.** Windows, Mac, and Chromebook all work. The training screen is built for a full-size screen.
-   **Chrome or Firefox.** Edge and Safari open the site too, but Teachable Machine itself recommends Chrome or Firefox, and its sound projects work best there.
-   **A webcam** (built in or USB). It's optional: every image project can also be trained from photo files you upload, so a phone camera plus a way to move photos to your computer is enough.
-   **A microphone** for the sound project in Module 2. Most laptops have one built in.

You do **not** need an account, a download, an install, or any money. Teachable Machine's own FAQ says it plainly: go to the site, click Get Started, and there's no need to log in.

:::note
Some lessons include short Python programs that draw charts. You don't need to read or run them. The chart, and the explanation under it, is the point.
:::

## Start your first project

1.  Open Chrome or Firefox and go to `teachablemachine.withgoogle.com`.
2.  Click **Get Started**. The **New Project** screen shows three cards: **Image Project**, **Audio Project**, and **Pose Project**.
3.  Click **Image Project**. You'll be asked to pick a model type.
4.  Choose **Standard image model** ("Best for most uses"). The other option, **Embedded image model**, makes tiny greyscale models for microcontrollers. We never need it.

The project page opens. On a first visit, little boxes labeled **1. Gather Samples**, **2. Train your Model**, and **3. Export your model** point at the three parts of the screen. They're the whole workflow in three steps:

```mermaid caption="The three panels of a Teachable Machine project, left to right"
flowchart LR
    A["Classes<br/>Class 1, Class 2, Add a class"] --> B["Training<br/>Train Model, Advanced"]
    B --> C["Preview<br/>Input, Output bars, Export Model"]
```

**Classes (left).** Each card is one category you want the model to learn, like "Thumbs up". A card starts with the label **Add Image Samples:** and two buttons, **Webcam** and **Upload**. Click the pencil next to "Class 1" to rename it. **Add a class** at the bottom makes another card.

**Training (middle).** The **Train Model** button turns your examples into a model. Under it, **Advanced** hides settings called Epochs, Batch Size, and Learning Rate. Leave them alone for now; Module 4 explains what each one does.

**Preview (right).** Until you train, it says "You must train a model on the left before you can preview it here." After training, it shows a live camera view and a bar for each class showing how strongly the model leans toward that class.

## Let Teachable Machine use your camera

Click **Webcam** on the Class 1 card. Your browser asks whether the site may use your camera. In Chrome, choose **Allow while visiting the site** (or **Allow this time**). In Firefox, click **Allow**. A live view of you appears inside the card with a **Hold to Record** button under it.

That's success. If instead you see "There was an error opening your webcam. Make sure permissions are enabled or switch to image uploading," the camera is blocked somewhere. There are two places it can be blocked: the browser, and the computer itself.

:::tabs
@tab Windows
**In the browser.** In Chrome, open the three-dot menu, then **Settings › Privacy and security › Site settings › Camera**. If `teachablemachine.withgoogle.com` is listed under "Not allowed", click it and change Camera to **Allow**. Reload the page.

**In Windows 11.** Open **Start › Settings › Privacy & security › Camera**. Turn on **Camera access** and **Let desktop apps access your camera**. Chrome and Firefox count as desktop apps, so the second switch is the one people miss.

**In Windows 10.** Open **Start › Settings › Privacy › Camera**. Make sure camera access for this device is on, then turn on **Allow desktop apps to access your camera**.

If a switch is greyed out, a school or family administrator controls it, and you'll need to ask them.
@tab macOS
**In the browser.** In Chrome, open the three-dot menu, then **Settings › Privacy and security › Site settings › Camera** and allow `teachablemachine.withgoogle.com`. In Firefox, click the camera icon at the left of the address bar and clear the block. Reload the page.

**In macOS.** Open the Apple menu, then **System Settings › Privacy & Security › Camera** (on older Macs: **System Preferences › Security & Privacy › Privacy › Camera**). Turn on the switch for your browser. macOS will ask you to quit and reopen the browser; do it, or the change won't apply.
@tab Chromebook
**In the browser.** Open the three-dot menu, then **Settings › Privacy and security › Site settings › Camera**, and allow `teachablemachine.withgoogle.com`. Reload the page.

**In ChromeOS.** Newer Chromebooks also have a system-wide switch. Open **Settings › Privacy and security › Privacy controls** and turn on **Camera access** (and **Microphone access** while you're there, for Module 2). Some Chromebooks also have a physical camera switch or slider on the side; check that it's on.

School Chromebooks are often managed by the school. If the camera is blocked and you can't change it, use **Upload** with photo files instead. Every activity in this course works that way.
:::

:::warning
**The camera works in other sites but not here.** Another app is probably holding it. Video-call apps (Zoom, Teams, Meet, FaceTime) keep the camera busy even when minimized. Quit them completely, then reload Teachable Machine. If it still fails, restart the browser.
:::

## Where your pictures go, and what would make them leave

This is worth knowing before you point a camera at your bedroom. Teachable Machine's FAQ makes three clear promises:

-   **Your samples stay in your browser tab.** Training happens on your own computer, "without sending anything to any servers."
-   **Closing the tab erases everything.** If you close the tab without saving, nothing is kept, in your browser or anywhere else.
-   **Two buttons change that.** **Save project to Drive** puts a copy of your samples in *your* Google Drive. **Upload my model** (in Export Model) publishes the trained model, but not your samples, at a link anyone who has it can use.

So the camera itself is not the privacy risk. What ends up *in the pictures* is. A few habits for this course:

-   Record against a plain wall or desk. Check the background for mail with your address, family photos, or a screen with messages on it.
-   Don't record other people without asking them first, and never record people who don't know they're being recorded.
-   Never click **Upload my model** in this course. Nothing here needs a public link.

Module 6 goes much further into privacy and consent. For now, those three habits cover everything you'll do.

## What to expect from this course

You won't write code here. You'll train real models in Teachable Machine, read charts that show how models behave, and work through thinking exercises that build judgement: is this even a machine learning problem, what will this model actually learn, can I trust this score? The questions get harder, but the tools stay this simple.

There's also an optional extra, Google Colab, for anyone curious about the Python behind the charts. Lesson 3 of this module shows how to open it. Nothing in the course requires it.

:::quiz
Q: You train a model on twenty photos of your room, then close the tab without saving anything. What happened to the photos?
- [ ] They're stored on Google's servers for next time
  Teachable Machine only sends samples anywhere if you save to Drive. Training happens in the tab.
- [x] They're gone; nothing was kept anywhere
  The FAQ says that if you close the tab without saving, nothing is saved in your browser or on any servers.
- [ ] They're in your browser's history
  Browser history records which pages you visited, not the pictures a page held in memory.

Q: Your friend's camera works on video calls but Teachable Machine shows "There was an error opening your webcam." Which fix should they try first?
- [ ] Reinstall the browser
  That's slow and rarely needed. Something simpler is usually holding the camera.
- [ ] Switch to the Embedded image model
  The model type doesn't change camera access at all.
- [x] Quit the video-call app completely, then reload the page
  A camera can usually be used by one app at a time, and call apps often keep it even when minimized.

Q: Which of these would put your work on the internet?
- [ ] Clicking Train Model
  Training runs inside your browser tab without sending anything to a server.
- [x] Clicking Upload my model in Export Model
  This publishes the trained model at a shareable link. Your samples stay private, but the model is public to anyone with the link.
- [ ] Renaming Class 1 to "Thumbs up"
  Class names live in the tab with everything else.
:::

:::takeaways
- Teachable Machine runs in Chrome or Firefox with no account, install, or cost.
- A project has three panels: Classes (your examples), Training, and Preview.
- A blocked camera is fixed in two places: the browser's site settings and the computer's privacy settings.
- Samples stay in your tab unless you save to Drive; closing the tab without saving erases them.
- Check what's in the background before you record.
:::
