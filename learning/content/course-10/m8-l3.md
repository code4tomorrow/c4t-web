- Present the robot in two minutes: behavior, states, evidence, and what's next.
- Assess the project against the rubric, and choose a next step after the course.
:::

"It works" is not a result. "It passed 10 of 10 tests, here are the log lines, and here's the regression we found and fixed on the way" is a result, and it's what separates a robotics project from a robot that happened to work once. This lesson turns your build into that kind of evidence.

## Step 1: Run the test plan

Here's a plan for the scanning avoider, written so every test can be run in Wokwi by moving the distance slider and pressing the button. Copy it, add rows for anything your robot does differently, and fill in the last column as you go. Line followers can start from the plan in the module 7 practice.

| ID | What you do | Pass if the log shows | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Start the simulation; don't press anything for 5 s | Only WAITING, with left and right at 0 | |
| T2 | Press START; after 2 s, drag slowly from 150 to 15 cm | BACKING_UP begins before `cm` drops below 15, then SCANNING | |
| T3 | In DRIVING, wiggle the slider between 19 and 22 for 5 s | No transition less than 400 ms after the previous one | |
| T4 | During the scan: 120 cm while it looks left, 40 while it looks right | `scan: left 120 cm, right 40` (give or take a centimeter), then a left turn: L-IN2 and R-IN3 lit | |
| T5 | Same, but 40 left and 120 right | A right turn: L-IN1 and R-IN4 lit | |
| T6 | Keep the slider at 15 through the scan and turn | TURNING lasts 3 s, then WAITING | |
| T7 | Once TURNING starts, slide to 100 | DRIVING again, no sooner than 400 ms into the turn | |
| T8 | Press the button during TURNING | WAITING on the very next log line, motors at 0 | |
| T9 | Press START and watch the first second | Motors stay at 0 for 1 s, then 160 | |
| T10 | In DRIVING, leave the slider at 80 for 4 s; repeat at 300 | STUCK at 80 cm; no STUCK at 300 cm | |

To record evidence, select the relevant lines in the Serial Monitor, copy them, and paste them under the test in your report. A test without a log excerpt counts as "not run".

:::tip
Run the plan from the top after every change, not just the test you were fixing. That full re-run is the regression test, and it's the only way to catch a fix that breaks something else.
:::

## Step 2: Write the iteration report

Each time a test fails and you change something, write one entry. Keep it short, and keep it honest: a failure you explain well is worth more than one you hide.

```text
ITERATION 2
Failed test:  T2-real (fast approach on the chassis)
Evidence:     log shows BACKING_UP at 18 cm, but the robot touched the box
Hypothesis:   median filter delay (about 150 ms) plus braking distance at CRUISE = 160
              is more than the 20 cm margin
One change:   STOP_CM 20 -> 28
Re-run:       T1 to T10 plus T2-real
Result:       T2-real now passes. T11 (drive through a 40 cm doorway) FAILS: the door
              frame enters the sensor's 15-degree beam inside 28 cm, so the robot
              refuses to go through
Next:         revert STOP_CM to 20; instead slow down near obstacles
              (CRUISE scales from 160 at 60 cm to 100 at 25 cm)
```

That's an example entry for a real chassis, and it shows why the full re-run matters: the fix for T2 broke T11, and a single-test check would never have shown it. Iteration 3 then fixes the cause (too much speed near obstacles) instead of the symptom.

A summary chart makes the story visible at a glance. Record each test's result per iteration, and let Python find the fixes and the regressions:

```python run alt="Stacked bar chart of 12 tests over three iterations: 11 pass in iteration 1, 11 in iteration 2, and all 12 in iteration 3" caption="Pass counts per iteration. The flat middle bar hides a fix and a regression, which the printout names."
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

results = {                       # one letter per iteration: P = pass, F = fail
    "T1": "PPP", "T2": "PPP", "T3": "PPP", "T4": "PPP", "T5": "PPP",
    "T6": "PPP", "T7": "PPP", "T8": "PPP", "T9": "PPP", "T10": "PPP",
    "T2-real": "FPP",
    "T11": "PFP",
}
iterations = len(next(iter(results.values())))

passes = [sum(r[i] == "P" for r in results.values()) for i in range(iterations)]
fails = [len(results) - p for p in passes]

for i in range(1, iterations):
    fixed = [t for t, r in results.items() if r[i - 1] == "F" and r[i] == "P"]
    broke = [t for t, r in results.items() if r[i - 1] == "P" and r[i] == "F"]
    print(f"Iteration {i + 1}: fixed {fixed or 'nothing'}, broke {broke or 'nothing'}")

labels = [f"Iteration {i + 1}" for i in range(iterations)]
plt.bar(labels, passes, color="#2b8a3e", label="pass")
plt.bar(labels, fails, bottom=passes, color="#e8590c", label="fail")
plt.ylabel(f"tests (of {len(results)})")
plt.legend()
```

The bar chart alone says iterations 1 and 2 were equally good. The printout says iteration 2 fixed one test and broke another, which is the real story. Put both in your report.

## Step 3: Present it in two minutes

A short screen recording of Wokwi (or a phone video of the chassis) with the Serial Monitor visible is the best format. Your computer has a recorder built in: Shift-Command-5 on a Mac, the Snipping Tool's record mode on Windows 11, and Screen capture on a Chromebook. Follow this outline:

| Time | Show | Say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 0:15 | The robot running | What it does, in one sentence |
| 0:15 to 0:45 | Your state diagram | The states, and the one transition you're proudest of |
| 0:45 to 1:30 | A live run with the log scrolling | One normal run, then one tricky test (T6 or T8 are good) |
| 1:30 to 2:00 | Your iteration chart | One failure, what you changed, and what you'd do next |

To share the simulation, use Wokwi's **Save** and then **Share** to get a link, the same way you saved projects in module 1. Before sharing, check that the project's name and description contain nothing personal.

## Step 4: The rubric

Score yourself honestly before you submit; then compare with your teacher's or a classmate's score. Each row is worth up to 4 points, for a total of 28.

| Criterion | 1: Beginning | 2: Developing | 3: Proficient | 4: Excellent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Circuit and pin plan | Circuit incomplete or pins conflict | Works, but no pin plan | Pin plan matches circuit; no conflicts | Plus clear labels, and real-hardware wiring notes |
| Sensor handling | Raw readings used directly | Filtered, but no-echo or lost-line becomes 0 | Filtered, with bad data handled on purpose | Plus calibration or fault-injection evidence |
| Motor control and safety | Motor logic scattered through the code | One drive function, but no speed limit or stop | Driver signals through `drive()`, S1 to S5 done | Plus ramping or proportional slow-down, all of S1 to S7 addressed |
| States and transitions | No explicit states | States exist but some have no exit | Diagram matches code; every state has an exit | Plus hysteresis and time limits that are justified by tests |
| Testing | A few informal tries | Tests listed but no pass criteria | 10 or more tests with criteria and log evidence | Plus edge cases and full re-runs after each change |
| Iteration report | No record of changes | Changes listed without evidence | Each change linked to a failed test and a re-run | Plus a regression found and explained |
| Communication | Hard to follow | Covers the behavior only | Behavior, states, evidence in about two minutes | Plus a clear "what I'd do next" grounded in results |

## Where to go next

Your robot uses the same loop as far bigger machines: sense, decide with explicit states, act, measure, adjust. Some directions from here:

- **Close the loop on speed.** Add wheel encoders and use the I and D terms of PID control, so the robot drives straight even when one motor is weaker.
- **Go wireless.** Wokwi also simulates the ESP32, which has Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, so the same robot logic can report its log to a web page.
- **Build the real one.** A two-wheel chassis kit, an L298N or TB6612FNG, and 4 × AA cells is enough to run your capstone sketch unchanged except for the numbers.
- **Join a team.** FIRST, VEX, World Robot Olympiad, and RoboCup Junior all run student robotics competitions, and every one of them rewards the test-and-iterate habits you used here.

:::takeaways
- A result is a test with a pass criterion and log evidence, not "it worked when I tried it".
- Re-run the whole plan after every change; regressions are only visible that way.
- An iteration report links a failed test, a hypothesis, one change, and a re-run.
- Present the behavior, the states, the evidence, and the next step, in about two minutes.
:::
