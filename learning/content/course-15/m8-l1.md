A recycling helper that says "blue bin" when you hold up a can. A laptop that taps you on the shoulder after ten minutes of slouching. A doorbell alert for someone wearing headphones. Each of these is a few hundred samples, one Teachable Machine model, and about a hundred lines of copied-and-edited code. You've already done every individual step. The capstone puts them together into one prototype that somebody else can try.

## The brief

Build an **interactive prototype** in which a Teachable Machine model (image, audio, or pose) controls something on screen. Your prototype must have:

1. **Approved data.** Every person in your samples agreed to be recorded, and anyone in a shared model agreed to sharing.
2. **A trained model** with a **Nothing** or **Background Noise** class, so it has an honest answer when nothing is happening.
3. **An unseen test** of at least 10 items per class, recorded in different conditions from training, with a confusion matrix.
4. **One repair round:** a written hypothesis about your biggest error, one targeted data change, and a retest on the same unseen items.
5. **An app** that turns predictions into actions using a threshold, a hold time, and a cooldown or re-arm.
6. **A model card** and a responsible export choice.
7. **A short live demo** that includes at least one failure you explain.

You'll hand in your project file (`.tm`), your model link or downloaded files, your sketch, your project log with test sheets, and your model card. The rubric is at the end of the third capstone lesson; read it now so nothing surprises you.

## Five project ideas

Pick one, or invent your own at a similar size. The difficulty is mostly about how alike the classes are and how fussy the app's rule is.

| Idea | Type | Classes | The hard part | Stretch goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gesture slide clicker** (starter) | Image | Next, Back, Nothing | A Nothing class that covers talking with your hands | A "Pause" gesture that blanks the screen |
| **Recycling helper screen** (starter) | Image | Recycling, Trash, Compost, Nothing | Enough different items per class; local recycling rules | "Not sure, ask a person" below the threshold |
| **Appliance or doorbell alert** (intermediate) | Audio | Background Noise, Doorbell, Microwave beep | Background noise from many rooms and times of day | A visible history of alerts with times |
| **Posture reminder** (intermediate) | Pose | Upright, Slouching, Away | Pose models care where you sit in the frame | A summary of upright minutes per session |
| **Fingerspelling practice helper** (advanced) | Image | Four to six letters, plus Nothing | Letters with similar handshapes, like A and S | Spaces after a long pause, and a "Not sure" display |

Two notes on the advanced idea. Leave out letters that need motion (in ASL, J and Z are drawn in the air), because a single frame can't show them. And be honest about what it is: a practice aid for a handful of letters, not a translator. Sign languages are full languages, with grammar carried by movement and facial expression. If you know someone who signs, ask them to try your helper and tell you what it gets wrong.

## Check your idea before you commit

Run three quick checks on paper. Each one has sunk real projects.

- **The one-frame test.** Can a single camera frame (or one second of sound) show the difference between every pair of classes? "Waving hello" versus "waving goodbye" fails: they look the same in a frame. "Thumbs up" versus "thumbs down" passes.
- **The people test.** Whose hands, faces, voices, or bodies will be in the data? If it recognizes *particular people*, change the idea. If other people appear, plan how you'll ask them, and what you'll do if they say no.
- **The cost test.** Which mistake would hurt your user more, a false positive or a false negative? Write it down now, before you have numbers to argue with.

Here's a filled-in plan for the doorbell idea, so you can see the level of detail that helps:

```text
Name: Doorbell for headphones
One sentence: When the doorbell rings, the laptop shows a big alert, so that
  someone wearing headphones knows to answer.
Project type: audio
Classes:
  - Background Noise: kitchen, hallway, TV, talking, dishwasher, quiet room
  - Doorbell: our doorbell, recorded from the hall, kitchen, and bedroom
  - Microwave beep: the microwave's finish beeps (so it stops being "Doorbell")
One-frame test: each sound fits in 1 second. Closest pair: Doorbell vs
  Microwave beep (both short electronic tones).
People and consent: voices of my family are in Background Noise. Recording:
  all agreed. Sharing: I'll keep the model downloaded, not uploaded.
Data plan: 40+ seconds of Background Noise from 4 rooms and 3 times of day;
  30 Doorbell clips and 30 Microwave clips, from 3 distances
Unseen test plan: 10 rings and 10 beeps on a different day, plus 10 minutes
  of evening TV and dinner noise
Costly error: false negative for Doorbell (a visitor is left outside)
App rule: threshold 0.8, hold one result, cooldown 10 s
Export: Download, so family voices in the background aren't in a public model
Biggest risk: dishwasher drowns out the bell. Backup: record bell clips with
  the dishwasher on in round 2
```

Notice the decisions it makes early: a third class to absorb a known false alarm, a low threshold because misses are the costly error, and a download instead of an upload because of who can be heard in the data.

## Milestones

Work in this order. Each milestone ends with something you can show, so if time runs out you still have a working, honest project.

```mermaid caption="Capstone milestones, each ending in something you can show"
timeline
    title Capstone milestones
    M1 Plan : Plan written : Consent collected
    M2 Data : Samples recorded in rounds : Model v1 trained
    M3 Test : Unseen test sheet : Confusion matrix
    M4 Repair : Hypothesis and one change : Retest, v2 logged
    M5 Export : Link or files : Model card
    M6 App : Rules tested on fake data : Play-test tally
    M7 Present : Polish : Demo with one explained failure
```

Two pieces of advice from every past version of a project like this:

- **Record the unseen test early (M3), and never touch it.** If you wait until the end, you'll be tempted to test on whatever's handy, which is usually training data.
- **Build the app (M6) with your v1 model's link if M4 runs long.** Because the app loads from a link, **Update my cloud model** swaps in the improved model later without changing any code.

:::tip
Keep everything in one folder: the `.tm` files for each version, your `test-set` folder, the project log, the model card, and a copy of your sketch. When something breaks the night before the demo, you'll know exactly which version last worked.
:::

## Before you start recording

Your plan is ready when you can answer yes to all of these:

- Every class is defined in terms of what the camera or microphone gets, and every pair passes the one-frame test.
- Every person who'll appear in the data has agreed, and you know whether they agreed to sharing.
- Your unseen test plan uses conditions that are *not* in your data plan.
- You've named the costly error, and your app rule leans the right way (a lower threshold if misses are worse, a higher one if false alarms are).
- You know whether you'll upload or download, and why.

If any answer is no, fix the plan first. Changing a plan takes five minutes; re-recording a dataset takes an afternoon.
