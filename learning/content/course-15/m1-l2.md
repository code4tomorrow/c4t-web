title: Train your first model in five minutes
summary: Open an Image Project, record two classes from your webcam, click Train Model, and watch the preview bars respond to your hand.
---

:::objectives
- Start a Standard image model project from the Teachable Machine home page.
- Find the three panels: classes on the left, Training in the middle, Preview on the right.
- Rename classes, record samples with **Hold to Record**, and train a model.
- Prove the model works by watching its preview bars change.
:::

The fastest way to understand Teachable Machine is to use it once from start to finish before learning why any of it works. You'll build the smallest useful model there is: one that answers a single question, *is there a thumbs-up in the picture or not?* It takes about five minutes, and the last minute is the fun one.

Here is the whole loop you're about to run. Every project in the course follows it.

```mermaid caption="The Teachable Machine loop, left to right on the screen"
flowchart LR
    A["Name your classes"] --> B["Record samples"]
    B --> C["Train Model"]
    C --> D["Preview: test it live"]
    D -->|"wrong answers?"| B
    D -->|"good enough"| E["Save or export"]
```

## Step 1: Open an image project

1. Go to **teachablemachine.withgoogle.com** and click **Get Started**.
2. The **New Project** page shows three cards: **Image Project**, **Audio Project**, and **Pose Project**. Below them are links to open an existing project from Google Drive or from a file, which you'll use in the next lesson.
3. Click **Image Project**. A box asks which kind of image model you want.
4. Choose **Standard image model** ("Best for most uses"). It works on 224 × 224 pixel color images.

The other choice, **Embedded image model**, makes a much smaller model from 96 × 96 grayscale images, meant for tiny microcontroller boards. You won't need it in this course.

## Step 2: Find your way around the screen

The project opens with three columns:

- **Left: your classes.** Two cards called **Class 1** and **Class 2**, each with **Add Image Samples:** and two buttons, **Webcam** and **Upload**. Below them is **Add a class**.
- **Middle: Training.** A **Train Model** button, and under it an **Advanced** section that you'll open in Module 3.
- **Right: Preview.** An **Export Model** button and a message: *"You must train a model on the left before you can preview it here."*

A **class** is one of the answers the model is allowed to give. This model gets two: thumbs up, and nothing.

## Step 3: Name the classes

Click the pencil icon next to **Class 1** and type `Thumbs up`. Then rename **Class 2** to `Nothing`.

Names don't change how the model learns, but they're what the preview shows, and a model with labels like "Class 1" is confusing a month later. Two classes can't share the same name; Teachable Machine refuses to train if they do.

## Step 4: Record the thumbs-up class

1. On the **Thumbs up** card, click **Webcam**. Allow the camera if the browser asks. A live square view of your camera appears.
2. Hold your thumb up in view. Press and **hold** the **Hold to Record** button. While you hold it, Teachable Machine grabs frames from the camera many times a second (the default setting is 24).
3. While recording, move a little: tilt your hand, bring it closer, pull it back, switch hands. Release after four or five seconds.

The card now says something like **87 Image Samples**, with a row of thumbnails. Click any thumbnail to see it larger; from there you can delete it or move it to another class.

:::tip
Sitting still while you record gives you 80 nearly identical photos, which teach the model about as much as 5 would. Small movements give it variety for free. Module 2 is all about this.
:::

## Step 5: Record the "nothing" class

On the **Nothing** card, click **Webcam** and record about the same number of samples with **no** thumbs-up: your face, your hand resting flat, an empty chair, your hand waving. Aim for a count within about 10 of the first class.

Why bother with a "nothing" class? Because a model can only answer with the classes you gave it. With only a **Thumbs up** class, every picture would be a thumbs-up, including an empty room. The **Nothing** class gives it somewhere else to put its answer.

## Step 6: Train

Click **Train Model**. The button changes to **Preparing training data...**, then **Training...**, and a moment later to **Model Trained**. On most laptops this takes well under a minute for a couple of hundred images.

:::warning
**Don't switch tabs while it trains.** Teachable Machine shows *"Don't switch tabs! You must leave this tab open to train your model."* Browsers slow down tabs you aren't looking at, so leaving can pause training or make it crawl. Stay on the page until the button reads **Model Trained**.
:::

## Step 7: Test it in the preview

The **Preview** panel wakes up as soon as training finishes. **Input** is switched **ON**, the webcam is selected, and under **Output** there's a bar for each class with a percentage.

Now try it. Hold up a thumbs-up, then put your hand down, then hold it up again. Here's what success looks like:

```text
Thumbs up visible:    Thumbs up ██████████████████  98%
                      Nothing   ▏                    2%

Hand down:            Thumbs up ▏                    1%
                      Nothing   ██████████████████  99%
```

The two percentages always add up to 100%, because the model is splitting its answer between your two classes. Module 4 explains what those numbers do and don't mean.

:::try
Make a thumbs-*down*. The model has never seen one, so it has to choose between the two answers it knows. Which bar wins, and by how much? Then try a fist, and a peace sign. Write down what happens; you'll explain it in Module 2.
:::

## No webcam? The upload path

On each class card, click **Upload** instead of **Webcam**. You'll see *"Choose images from your files, or drag & drop here"*. Drag in .png or .jpg photos; Teachable Machine crops each to a square. Twenty or more photos per class is enough for this test.

To test, switch the preview's input from **Webcam** to **File** using the menu beside the **Input** toggle, and upload a photo the model hasn't seen.

## When it doesn't work

- **"There was an error opening your webcam."** Permission is blocked, or another app has the camera. Go back to the permission steps in the previous lesson, then reload.
- **Train Model is grayed out or shows an error.** Every class that is switched on needs at least one sample, and an empty card blocks training. Check both cards for a sample count.
- **Training seems frozen.** You probably switched tabs. Return to the tab and wait, or click **Train Model** again.
- **The preview is stuck on one class no matter what you do.** Usually the two classes look too similar, or one has far more samples than the other. Record more varied samples for the weaker class and train again.

:::quiz
Q: You train a model with a **Thumbs up** class and a **Nothing** class, then show it a thumbs-down. What will the bars show?
- [ ] Two empty bars, because a thumbs-down isn't a class
  The model never abstains. It always splits 100% across the classes you gave it.
- [x] Some split of 100% between Thumbs up and Nothing
  It has to pick from what it knows. A thumbs-down shares a lot with a thumbs-up (a fist, one finger out), so the result can go either way.
- [ ] A new bar labeled "Unknown"
  Teachable Machine only shows the classes you created.

Q: Why record the thumbs-up while moving your hand, instead of holding perfectly still?
- [x] Varied samples teach the model what stays the same about a thumbs-up across positions
  Identical frames add almost no information. Different angles and distances help it recognize the gesture anywhere in the frame.
- [ ] Moving makes training finish faster
  Training time depends on how many samples you have, not how varied they are.
- [ ] Teachable Machine deletes frames that are too similar
  It keeps every frame you record.
:::

:::takeaways
- **Get Started › Image Project › Standard image model** opens a project with classes on the left, Training in the middle, and Preview on the right.
- **Hold to Record** captures frames while you hold the button; moving while you record gives you variety.
- A model can only answer with the classes you made, so a "nothing" class matters.
- Stay on the tab while it trains, then test live in **Preview**.
:::
