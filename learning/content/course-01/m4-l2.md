---
title: Draw a flower of squares with loops
summary: Use Scratch's Pen extension to draw a square the long way, shrink it with a loop, turn it into any shape by changing one number, repeat it into a twelve-petal flower, and draw a spiral that stops at the edge.
---

:::objectives
- Add the Pen extension so a sprite leaves a line wherever it moves.
- Draw a square with repeated blocks, then replace them with a loop.
- Turn a square into any regular shape by working out the turn angle.
- Put a loop inside another loop to repeat a whole shape.
- Use `repeat until` to stop on a condition instead of a count.
:::

Loops are hard to see in most programs, because the computer repeats things so quickly. Drawing fixes that. Give the cat a pen, and every pass through a loop leaves a line on the stage, so you can count the passes with your eyes.

## What we're building

By the end, one click of the green flag draws twelve overlapping squares arranged in a ring, each one a different color, like the petals of a flower. Pressing **S** clears the stage and draws a square spiral that grows until it hits the edge.

The flower is a loop inside a loop. Here's its shape:

```mermaid caption="The flower: an outer loop of 12 petals, and an inner loop of 4 sides for each petal"
flowchart TD
    A[Clear the stage, reset, pen down] --> B{12 petals drawn?}
    B -->|no| C[Change the pen color]
    C --> D{4 sides drawn?}
    D -->|no| E[Move 100, turn right 90]
    E --> D
    D -->|yes| F[Turn right 30]
    F --> B
    B -->|yes| G[Done]
```

## Step 1: Add the Pen extension and set up

Start a new project (**File** › **New**). At the very bottom-left of the editor, under the block categories, click the **Add Extension** button. Choose **Pen**. A new dark-green **Pen** category appears at the bottom of the list.

The cat is big enough to hide the lines it draws, so shrink it, and build this setup script:

```scratch
when green flag clicked
set size to (30) %
erase all
pen up
go to x: (0) y: (0)
point in direction (90)
set pen size to (3)
pen down
```

`erase all` wipes the previous drawing. `pen down` means "draw as you move" and `pen up` means "move without drawing".

Why `pen up` *before* `go to`? Because state carries over between runs. Say you stopped the last drawing halfway, so the cat is off to one side with its pen still down. Jumping to the center would draw a long stray line across your fresh, empty stage. Lifting the pen first, then moving, then putting it down is the drawing version of setting a robot's starting state.

Click the flag. Nothing visible happens yet, apart from the cat shrinking and moving to the center. That's correct: you've set the state, and nothing has moved with the pen down.

## Step 2: A square, the long way

Add four pairs of blocks under the setup:

```scratch
move (100) steps
turn right (90) degrees
move (100) steps
turn right (90) degrees
move (100) steps
turn right (90) degrees
move (100) steps
turn right (90) degrees
```

Click the flag. The cat draws a square, going right, then down, then left, then up, and ends where it started, facing right again. Count the blocks you just added: eight, made of the same two blocks four times.

## Step 3: Spot the pattern and loop it

Delete those eight blocks (drag them back to the palette) and replace them with a `repeat` from **Control**, with one copy of the pair inside its jaws:

```scratch
repeat (4)
move (100) steps
turn right (90) degrees
end
```

Click the flag. You get the same square from three blocks instead of eight. Now try changing `100` to `60`. One edit resizes the whole square; in the long version you'd have needed four edits, all exactly right.

## Step 4: One number changes the shape

What if you want a triangle, or a hexagon? Here's the idea. By the time the cat has drawn the whole shape and is back where it started, facing the same way, it has turned all the way around exactly once. All the way around is 360 degrees. So each turn has to be:

**turn = 360 ÷ number of sides**

| Shape | Sides (`repeat`) | Turn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Triangle | 3 | 120 |
| Square | 4 | 90 |
| Pentagon | 5 | 72 |
| Hexagon | 6 | 60 |
| Octagon | 8 | 45 |
| Almost a circle | 36 | 10 |

Try three or four rows of the table, changing both numbers in your loop each time. For the 36-sided shape, shrink `move` to about `10` steps so it fits on the stage.

:::warning
**A triangle that won't close.** If you remember that a triangle's corners are 60 degrees and use `turn right (60) degrees`, the cat draws three sides of a hexagon and stops with a gap. The cat turns by the angle *outside* each corner, not the angle inside it. Use 360 ÷ 3 = 120.
:::

Put the loop back to a square (`repeat (4)`, `move (100) steps`, `turn right (90) degrees`) before the next step.

## Step 5: A loop inside a loop

One square is one petal. A flower is twelve petals, each turned a little from the last. So repeat the *whole square* 12 times, turning 30 degrees after each one (12 × 30 = 360, all the way around again).

Drag a second `repeat` block so its jaws wrap around your square loop, and add a turn after the inner loop but still inside the outer one:

```scratch
repeat (12)
repeat (4)
move (100) steps
turn right (90) degrees
end
turn right (30) degrees
end
```

Click the flag and watch. The inner loop draws a square; the outer loop turns the cat and starts the next square.

How many times does `move (100) steps` run? The inner loop runs it 4 times, and the outer loop runs the inner loop 12 times, so it's 12 × 4 = **48** lines from just six blocks.

## Step 6: Color each petal

From the **Pen** category, add `change pen (color v) by (8)` inside the outer loop, just above the inner loop:

```scratch
repeat (12)
change pen (color v) by (8)
repeat (4)
move (100) steps
turn right (90) degrees
end
turn right (30) degrees
end
```

Because it's inside the outer loop but outside the inner one, the color changes once per petal, not once per side. Move it inside the inner loop and every *side* gets its own color instead. Try both and see the difference; where a block sits decides how often it runs.

## Step 7: A spiral that stops at the edge

Every loop so far has known its count in advance. This one won't. A square spiral draws a side, turns, and makes the next side a bit longer, over and over. How many sides fit before it reaches the edge of the stage? You could work it out, but you don't have to: let the loop check.

Make a variable called `side` (**Variables** › **Make a Variable**). Then build this second script next to the first:

```scratch
when [s v] key pressed
erase all
pen up
go to x: (0) y: (0)
point in direction (90)
set [side v] to (5)
pen down
repeat until <touching (edge v)?>
move (side) steps
turn right (90) degrees
change [side v] by (5)
end
pen up
say [Hit the edge!] for (2) seconds
```

The `touching` block is in **Sensing**; click its dropdown and choose **edge**. Press **S**. The spiral grows outward, and the moment the cat touches the edge, the loop stops and the cat speaks.

:::try
Change `change [side v] by (5)` to `change [side v] by (0)` and press **S**. The cat traces the same tiny square forever, because nothing in the loop ever gets it closer to the edge. Click the red stop sign, then put the `5` back.
:::

## The finished program

```scratch
when green flag clicked
set size to (30) %
erase all
pen up
go to x: (0) y: (0)
point in direction (90)
set pen size to (3)
pen down
repeat (12)
change pen (color v) by (8)
repeat (4)
move (100) steps
turn right (90) degrees
end
turn right (30) degrees
end
```

```scratch
when [s v] key pressed
erase all
pen up
go to x: (0) y: (0)
point in direction (90)
set [side v] to (5)
pen down
repeat until <touching (edge v)?>
move (side) steps
turn right (90) degrees
change [side v] by (5)
end
pen up
say [Hit the edge!] for (2) seconds
```

Save it as `m4-square-flower.sb3`.

:::quiz
Q: You want a pentagon (5 sides). What should the turn be?
- [x] 72 degrees
  360 ÷ 5 = 72. After five turns of 72, the cat has turned exactly once around and the shape closes.
- [ ] 108 degrees
  That's the angle *inside* each corner of a pentagon. The cat turns by the outside angle, so 108 makes the lines cross into a star-like tangle that doesn't close after 5 sides.
- [ ] 60 degrees
  Five turns of 60 is only 300 degrees, so the shape is left with a gap. 60 is the turn for a hexagon.

Q: In the flower, how many times does `turn right (90) degrees` run?
- [ ] 12
  That's how many times the outer loop runs. Each outer pass runs the inner loop's 4 turns.
- [ ] 16
  That adds the counts (12 + 4). Nested loops multiply: every outer pass runs the whole inner loop.
- [x] 48
  12 outer passes × 4 inner passes = 48 turns of 90 degrees, plus 12 separate turns of 30.

Q: What goes wrong if you remove `pen up` from the start of the flower script?
- [x] If a run was stopped halfway, the next run draws a stray line to the center
  The pen is still down from last time, so the `go to` jump draws a line from wherever the cat stopped. Lifting the pen first resets that piece of state.
- [ ] The flower is drawn in the wrong color
  Color comes from the pen color blocks. `pen up` only decides whether moving draws.
- [ ] Nothing is drawn at all
  `pen down` comes later in the script, so the flower still draws.
:::

:::takeaways
- Find the repeating unit, write it once inside `repeat`, and change one number instead of many.
- For a regular shape, the turn is 360 ÷ the number of sides.
- A loop inside a loop multiplies: 12 passes of a 4-pass loop runs the inner body 48 times.
- Where a block sits (before, inside the inner loop, inside the outer loop, after) decides how often it runs.
- Use `repeat until` when you can't easily know the count, and make sure the body moves toward the stopping condition.
:::

## Ideas to extend it

-   Ask "How many sides?" and draw that shape using `(360) / (answer)` for the turn.
-   Make the flower from triangles or hexagons instead of squares. What does the outer loop's turn need to be for 18 petals?
-   Use the pen's `stamp` block to leave copies of the cat around the ring instead of drawing lines.
