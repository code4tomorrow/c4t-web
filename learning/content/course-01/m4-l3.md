## Warm-up: find the unit

What's the repeating unit in each sequence, and how many times does it repeat?

```text
A.  red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue
B.  1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3, 1, 2
```

:::solution
**A** is *red, blue, blue*, repeated 3 times.

**B** is *1, 2, 3*, repeated 2 times, followed by an extra *1, 2*. It doesn't fit evenly, and that's fine: a loop of 2 passes handles the repeating part, and two extra steps after the loop handle the leftover. Real tasks are often like this, with a pattern plus a few odd steps at one end.
:::

### Exercise 1: Compress the dance

Here's a dance routine, written out in full:

```text
clap, clap, stomp, spin, clap, clap, stomp, spin, clap, clap, stomp, spin, bow
```

Rewrite it as a loop. Then look inside your loop's body: is there a smaller pattern you could loop too?

:::hint
Find where the routine starts to repeat itself. Is the last move part of the pattern?
:::

:::solution
```text
repeat 3 times:
    clap, clap, stomp, spin
bow
```

The bow happens once, at the end, so it goes after the loop. Inside the body, *clap, clap* is a tiny pattern of its own:

```text
repeat 3 times:
    repeat 2 times:
        clap
    stomp, spin
bow
```

Both versions are correct. For two claps, the first is easier to read. If the routine had ten claps in a row, the inner loop would be worth it.
:::

### Exercise 2: Before, inside, or after?

The school office needs labels for 300 students. These are the steps, in no particular order:

```text
Turn off the printer.             Peel off the label.
Type the student's name.          Load a new label roll.
Stick the label on a badge.       Turn on the printer.
Press Print.                      Put all the badges in a box for the front desk.
```

Write the plan as a loop. Put each step before, inside, or after the loop, in the right order.

:::hint
For each step, ask: "Does this happen once per student, or once for the whole job?"
:::

:::solution
```text
Before the loop:
    1. Turn on the printer.
    2. Load a new label roll.
For each of the 300 students:
    3. Type the student's name.
    4. Press Print.
    5. Peel off the label.
    6. Stick the label on a badge.
After the loop:
    7. Put all the badges in a box for the front desk.
    8. Turn off the printer.
```

The four steps inside the loop happen 300 times each; the other four happen once. Steps 7 and 8 could swap without causing a problem. Did you notice a hidden question, too? One label roll probably doesn't hold 300 labels. A real plan needs a check inside the loop for an empty roll, and Module 5 is about adding decisions like that.
:::

### Exercise 3: Counting or condition?

For each job, decide whether you'd use a counting loop or a condition-controlled loop, and say why in a few words.

```text
a. Water each of the 8 plants on the windowsill.
b. Fill the bath until the water reaches the line.
c. Shuffle the deck until the top card is an ace.
d. Do 3 sets of 10 push-ups.
e. Keep reading pages until you find the chapter about sharks.
f. Print a badge for every name on a list of 300.
```

:::hint
Before the job starts, could you write down exactly how many passes it'll take? If yes, it's a counting loop.
:::

:::solution
| Job | Loop | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a | Counting | 8 plants, known in advance |
| b | Condition | You stop when the water reaches the line, however long it takes |
| c | Condition | It might take 1 shuffle or 20 |
| d | Counting, twice | A loop of 3 sets, each with a loop of 10: a nested counting loop |
| e | Condition | You don't know which page the chapter starts on |
| f | Counting | One pass per name, and the list says how many |
:::

### Exercise 4: Predict the nested loop

Don't run this yet. Predict how many times the cat says each word, and how long the whole script takes.

```scratch
when green flag clicked
repeat (2)
say [Ready] for (1) seconds
repeat (3)
say [Go!] for (0.5) seconds
end
end
say [Finished] for (1) seconds
```

Then build it and check, using a clock or by counting out loud.

:::hint
Make a table with one row per pass of the *outer* loop, and write down every word said in that pass. Then add up the times.
:::

:::solution
```text
Outer pass 1:  Ready, Go!, Go!, Go!
Outer pass 2:  Ready, Go!, Go!, Go!
After:         Finished
```

-   **Ready:** 2 times (once per outer pass).
-   **Go!:** 6 times (3 per outer pass × 2 passes).
-   **Finished:** once, after both loops.

Time: each outer pass takes 1 + 3 × 0.5 = 2.5 seconds, so two passes take 5 seconds. Add 1 second for "Finished": **6 seconds** in total.
:::

### Exercise 5: Draw a staircase

Use the Pen extension to draw a staircase of 5 steps going up and to the right. Each step is 30 steps wide and 30 steps tall. Start the pen at x `-150`, y `-150`, facing right. The cat should finish at the center of the stage.

```text
The drawing (each step is 30 wide, 30 tall):

                    _|
                 _|
              _|
           _|
        _|
```

:::hint
Find the repeating unit by drawing one stair with your finger: along, up, and back to facing along. That needs two moves and two turns. Remember to set the starting state with the pen up.
:::

:::solution
```scratch
when green flag clicked
set size to (30) %
erase all
pen up
go to x: (-150) y: (-150)
point in direction (90)
pen down
repeat (5)
move (30) steps
turn left (90) degrees
move (30) steps
turn right (90) degrees
end
```

Each pass moves the cat 30 to the right and 30 up, so after 5 passes it's at x -150 + 150 = 0 and y -150 + 150 = 0, the center. The `turn right` at the end of the body puts the cat back to facing right, ready for the next stair. Leave it out, and the second pass heads off to the left.
:::

### Exercise 6: The rocket countdown

Make the cat count down from 10 to 1, saying each number for 1 second, then say "Liftoff!". Use a variable called `count` and a `repeat until` loop.

```text
Cat: 10
Cat: 9
...
Cat: 1
Cat: Liftoff!
```

Once it works, answer this: what does the cat say if you swap the two blocks inside your loop?

:::hint
Set `count` to 10 before the loop. Inside the body, say the number, then make it one smaller. When should the loop stop?
:::

:::solution
```scratch
when green flag clicked
set [count v] to (10)
repeat until <(count) = (0)>
say (count) for (1) seconds
change [count v] by (-1)
end
say [Liftoff!] for (2) seconds
```

Trace the last two passes. With `count` at 1, the loop checks "is it 0?", gets no, says 1, and changes `count` to 0. On the next check the answer is yes, so the loop stops before saying 0.

If you swap the two blocks, the number goes down *before* it's said, so the cat says 9, 8, ..., 1, **0**, and never says 10. Same blocks, different order, off by one at both ends.

A `repeat (10)` loop would also work here, since you know the count in advance. `repeat until` is a better fit for the way people describe a countdown: keep going until you reach zero.
:::

### Challenge: The any-shape machine

Make the cat ask "How many sides?" and then draw a regular shape with that many sides. The shape should stay about the same size whatever number you type, so the total length of all the sides should always be 300 steps.

Test it with 3, 4, 6, and 40. Then try 2 and 1, and explain what you see.

:::hint
The answer to the question can go straight into a `repeat` block. You need two calculations: the length of each side (300 shared among the sides) and the turn (360 shared among the corners). Both are divisions using `answer`.
:::

:::solution
```scratch
when green flag clicked
ask [How many sides?] and wait
erase all
pen up
go to x: (0) y: (0)
point in direction (90)
pen down
repeat (answer)
move ((300) / (answer)) steps
turn right ((360) / (answer)) degrees
end
```

With 40 sides, each side is 7.5 steps and each turn is 9 degrees, which looks like a circle. With **2**, the cat moves 150 steps, turns 180 degrees, and comes straight back: a "shape" with two sides is a line drawn twice. With **1**, it heads right for 300 steps (the edge of the stage stops it before then) and turns all the way around to face the same way: just a line. The program follows its rule correctly; the inputs are what don't make sense as shapes. Inputs like these are edge cases, and Module 5 is about handling them on purpose.
:::

## Watch a loop inside a loop run

If Exercise 4 or the staircase felt slippery, this helps. A theater has 2 rows with 3 seats each, and this program prints a ticket for every seat. Step through it and watch `row` and `seat`. The inner loop runs all of its passes for every single pass of the outer loop, so `seat` goes 1, 2, 3 while `row` stays at 1, and only then does `row` become 2.

:::trace Printing a ticket for every seat
```python
for row in [1, 2]:
    for seat in [1, 2, 3]:
        print("Ticket: row", row, "seat", seat)
print("All tickets printed.")
```
:::
